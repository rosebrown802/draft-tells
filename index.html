Marked proof

What each mark means

Dashes
Em dashes, counted. One or two per few hundred words reads as style. More reads as a tic.
Threes
Lists of exactly three. Aim for one per section at most. Two items or four read as more human.
Pivots
"It isn't X. It's Y," "not just X," and "X, not Y." Keep the best one or two in a piece and state the rest plainly.
Stock phrases
Words that turn up in machine prose far more than in people's: tapestry, testament, delve, at its core.
One-liners
A short sentence standing alone as a paragraph, used as a moral. One lands. Five is a pattern.
Quick questions
A question of a few words that the next sentence answers. "The result?"
Echoes
The same run of six or more words appearing twice in the draft.
Draft Tells checks habits, not authorship. It cannot tell who or what wrote a piece, and plenty of good human writing uses every pattern here. Use the marks to decide what to keep on purpose.
