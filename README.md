# Draft Tells

Paste a draft and see the writing habits that make prose read as machine-made, marked where they occur.

**Live tool:** https://rosebrown802.github.io/draft-tells/

Your draft never leaves your browser. There is no server, no account and no tracking.

## The problem

Writers and marketing teams now draft with AI help, and readers have learned to spot it. The giveaways are habits: too many em dashes, lists of exactly three, "it isn't X, it's Y," and a handful of stock phrases. Hunting for them by eye in a long draft is slow, and they are easy to miss in your own work.

## What it marks

| Mark | What it catches |
|---|---|
| Dashes | Em dashes, counted against the length of the draft |
| Threes | Lists of exactly three |
| Pivots | "It isn't X. It's Y," "not just X," and "X, not Y" |
| Stock phrases | Words far more common in machine prose: tapestry, testament, delve, at its core |
| One-liners | A short sentence standing alone as a paragraph |
| Quick questions | A question of a few words that the next sentence answers |
| Adverbs | Words ending in -ly, plus qualifiers such as just, very, really and quite |
| Echoes | The same run of six or more words appearing twice |

Each habit gets a count, a rate per 1,000 words and a rating of Fine, Watch or Heavy. Select a tile to isolate one habit in the marked-up draft.

## What it is not

Draft Tells checks habits. It cannot tell who or what wrote a piece, and good human writing uses every pattern here. The marks are for deciding what to keep on purpose.

## How it was built

I am a writer and creative director, and I edit AI-assisted drafts back into a human voice as part of my work. The rules come from that editing. I designed the tool and its rules and built it with Claude as my coding partner. It is a single HTML file with no dependencies.
